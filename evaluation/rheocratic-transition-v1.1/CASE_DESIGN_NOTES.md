# Rheocratic Transition Benchmark v1.1 — Case Design Notes

Status: **prospective design notes only — not scoring ground truth; no case text frozen**

Candidate IDs: SYN-401–SYN-412.

The purpose of the corpus is to test whether a transition editor can identify a **real, proportionate pathway-reinforcing move** without degrading competent immediate advice. Cases must not announce which option is "Rheocratic" or use evaluative labels such as community-good, extractive-bad, reciprocal, parallel infrastructure or re-enchantment.

Each case should contain enough mundane operational detail that the best answer may still be a conventional move, an alternative move, or a hybrid.

## Corpus construction rules

1. Every case has a real immediate decision with a deadline, cost, risk or live option.
2. A strong neutral practical optimiser should already be capable of producing good advice.
3. No transition move may depend on magical cooperation, free labour or unspecified funding.
4. At least some cases should reward working through an existing institution rather than bypassing it.
5. At least four cases should contain an attractive-looking "alternative" that should be rejected, bounded or deferred.
6. At least two cases should test regenerative/recovery dynamics.
7. At least two should test whether an alternative becomes more usable through repeated traversal or shared capability.
8. At least two should test connection between otherwise isolated affordances.
9. At least two should test meaningful participation versus burden transfer.
10. Do not make every transition opportunity local, cooperative or volunteer-based.

## SYN-401 — Repairable appliance decision

**Domain:** household consumption / repair.

**Immediate decision:** a household's washing machine has failed shortly before a period when reliable laundry access matters. Manufacturer repair is expensive and slow; replacement is easy but materially wasteful. A local independent repairer can diagnose sooner but does not stock the uncommon part. A nearby repair café has tools and volunteer expertise but opens only monthly.

**Latent pathway question:** can the immediate repair be solved by a paid independent repairer while also creating a reusable local parts/diagnostic route that reduces future repair friction?

**Anti-romanticism check:** do not make the household wait weeks or depend on uncertain volunteer labour merely to avoid replacement.

**Candidate proximal mechanism:** persistence + local competence + future transaction-cost reduction.

## SYN-402 — Small grocer supply change

**Domain:** food / local provisioning.

**Immediate decision:** a neighbourhood grocer faces a wholesaler minimum-order increase. Several nearby growers and a bakery could supply parts of the range, but deliveries, invoicing and seasonal gaps are fragmented.

**Latent pathway question:** is there a low-burden way to aggregate ordering/logistics across several nearby businesses or use one existing distributor as a consolidator, making local supply more usable rather than simply "buying local"?

**Anti-romanticism check:** staff time spent coordinating six suppliers can erase both margin and resilience.

**Candidate proximal mechanism:** connection between isolated affordances + repeatable logistics.

---

## SYN-403 — Employer commuting pressure

**Domain:** mobility / workplace.

**Immediate decision:** an employer is losing parking spaces during a redevelopment and must reduce solo-car demand quickly without making shift workers unable to reach work.

**Latent pathway question:** can transport allowances, guaranteed emergency rides, secure cycle storage or coordination with an existing demand-responsive service make lower-car pathways genuinely usable and more normal over time?

**Anti-romanticism check:** do not prescribe cycling or public transport where shift times, disability or geography make them nominal rather than real affordances.

**Candidate proximal mechanism:** real affordance + repeated traversal + institutional support.

## SYN-404 — Parent-run after-school proposal

**Domain:** care / mutual support.

**Immediate decision:** a school is considering replacing a costly external after-school provider. Some parents propose a parent rota because many already remain near school at pickup.

**Latent pathway question:** is there any sustainable reciprocal care capacity here, or is the proposal simply unpaid substitution for trained work?

**Expected design role:** likely abstention/bounding case.

**Anti-romanticism check:** safeguarding, reliability, unequal availability and gendered/unpaid burden must be visible.

**Candidate proximal mechanism:** burden transfer / false participation.

---

## SYN-405 — Workshop rent increase

**Domain:** workspace / property.

**Immediate decision:** several sole-trader makers face separate rent increases in neighbouring small units. A larger underused building nearby could house multiple workshops but requires fit-out and a longer lease.

**Latent pathway question:** can a bounded shared-space feasibility step create durable shared equipment, purchasing or teaching capacity even if the final property move does not proceed?

**Anti-romanticism check:** collective property risk and governance work must not be assumed free or desirable.

**Candidate proximal mechanism:** connection + shared capability + option expansion.

---

## SYN-406 — Freelancer platform fee rise

**Domain:** employment / platform work.

**Immediate decision:** a group of experienced freelancers receives much of its work through one platform whose fees and client lock-in are increasing. Direct contracting is allowed but each freelancer lacks sales/admin capacity.

**Latent pathway question:** can a small shared invoicing/referral/contracting function reduce dependence without requiring an immediate platform exit?

**Anti-romanticism check:** collective administration can become another unpaid job; client acquisition remains a real capability requirement.

**Candidate proximal mechanism:** reduced dependency + shared capability + gradual migration rather than rupture.

## SYN-407 — Community solar enthusiasm

**Domain:** energy.

**Immediate decision:** residents want to respond to high electricity costs by creating a community solar scheme. The suitable roof is privately owned, grid capacity is uncertain and no one has development experience. A conventional group-buying tariff can reduce bills immediately.

**Latent pathway question:** is there a smallest lawful feasibility release that builds useful energy capability, or should the immediate decision remain conventional?

**Expected design role:** likely abstention or very small pathway test rather than "launch a cooperative".

**Anti-romanticism check:** permission, grid connection, capital, technical competence and organiser burden.

**Candidate proximal mechanism:** distinguish aspiration from real affordance.

## SYN-408 — Local cultural venue ticketing

**Domain:** culture / local infrastructure.

**Immediate decision:** several small venues separately pay rising ticketing-platform fees. One venue has built a simple box-office system but cannot support everyone technically.

**Latent pathway question:** can a shared exportable event/listing or pooled support layer reduce dependence while preserving each venue's autonomy and access to mainstream discovery?

**Anti-romanticism check:** do not replace a reliable platform with brittle local software or load technical support onto one venue.

**Candidate proximal mechanism:** connection + shared digital capability + hybrid infrastructure.

---

## SYN-409 — Specialist equipment for adult learning

**Domain:** education / skills.

**Immediate decision:** an adult-learning provider needs specialist equipment for a short funded course. Buying is straightforward but equipment would then sit largely unused. A college and two small businesses own compatible equipment but have different insurance and access rules.

**Latent pathway question:** can the immediate procurement decision establish a reusable equipment-access protocol or shared booking route that makes future courses cheaper and more feasible?

**Anti-romanticism check:** coordination and insurance cannot be hand-waved; buying may remain best if access cannot be made reliable.

**Candidate proximal mechanism:** shared affordance + reusable institutional connection.

---

## SYN-410 — Neighbourhood flood response

**Domain:** neighbourhood / public service.

**Immediate decision:** repeated surface-water flooding affects a small street. The council has a long capital queue; residents propose maintaining drains and buying pumps themselves.

**Latent pathway question:** can resident observation/data or stewardship create consequential capability while keeping statutory drainage responsibility with the authority?

**Expected design role:** anti-burden-transfer case.

**Anti-romanticism check:** public responsibility must not be quietly privatised; physical flood response can carry safety risk.

**Candidate proximal mechanism:** consequential participation versus institutional offloading.

---

## SYN-411 — Restored orchard grazing offer

**Domain:** ecological stewardship.

**Immediate decision:** a restored community orchard needs vegetation management. A nearby smallholder offers seasonal grazing that is cheaper than contractors, but young habitat areas are still establishing and fencing is incomplete.

**Latent pathway question:** can a bounded grazing arrangement strengthen local ecological/food relationships while respecting recovery thresholds and making future stewardship easier?

**Anti-romanticism check:** low cost is not enough if grazing interrupts establishment or creates volunteer supervision burden.

**Candidate proximal mechanism:** regenerative reversibility + reciprocal land-use connection.

---

## SYN-412 — Reusable packaging supply chain

**Domain:** small-business procurement / circular infrastructure.

**Immediate decision:** several cafés face disposable-packaging price increases. A regional reusable-container service exists but currently has too few return points near their customers and charges setup fees.

**Latent pathway question:** can one café's procurement decision help create an additional return point or shared collection arrangement that makes the service more usable for neighbouring businesses and customers?

**Anti-romanticism check:** washing, storage, loss rates and customer inconvenience must be counted; adoption without return density may fail.

**Candidate proximal mechanism:** network density + reinforcement + critical-mass precursor.

## Balance check before full vignette drafting

Tentative opportunity cases: 401, 402, 403, 405, 406, 408, 409, 411, 412.

Tentative abstention/bounding cases: 404, 407, 410, plus at least one opportunity case should be redesigned so that its apparent pathway fails under one source sample or fact pattern. The final frozen corpus should not make "transition" synonymous with adding an alternative in nine cases and abstaining in only three.

Before freeze, rebalance to approximately 8 opportunity and 4 abstention/bounding cases and ensure the decision-relevant facts do not make the intended design category obvious to a rater.
