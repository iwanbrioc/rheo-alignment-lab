# Contributing an unseen historical case

This is a research workflow, not a request to upload confidential files to GitHub.

## What a contributor provides privately

A useful case has four parts:

1. **T0 — what was known before the decision.** Prefer contemporaneous emails, notes, proposals, minutes, budgets, messages, logs or reports.
2. **T1 — what was actually done.** Record the decision/action and the rationale documented at the time.
3. **T2 — what happened afterwards.** Record later consequences, mechanisms, affected parties and genuinely available alternatives.
4. **Permission record.** Confirm that the contributor has authority to provide the material for this research use and that unnecessary personal data has been removed.

## Privacy rules

- Do not commit real case content to the public Rheo GitHub repository.
- Remove names, email addresses, phone numbers and other unnecessary identifiers from T0 before it is sent to a model provider.
- Do not include sensitive personal data unless it is genuinely necessary to understand the decision and there is a lawful/ethical basis to use it.
- Keep raw source documents in the contributor-controlled private case directory.
- The model-facing T0 brief should use pseudonymous roles where possible (for example, `programme lead`, `supplier`, `trustee`, `partner organisation`).
- A combination of structural details can itself identify a case. Pseudonymisation reduces but does not eliminate re-identification risk.

## Blinding

The person constructing T0 should avoid reading or importing later T2 material while drafting it where practical. Once T0/T1/T2 are frozen, the model-analysis operator should only have access to T0 until all Rheo/control outputs are complete and hashed/frozen.

Human raters should receive shuffled maps with condition labels removed. They should not see T1/T2 until the scoring task requires the frozen target key.

## What makes a good first pilot

Choose a case that is:

- non-public/non-indexed;
- bounded around one real decision;
- supported by a manageable set of contemporaneous records;
- not acutely sensitive or legally contentious;
- consequential enough that later mechanisms can be described;
- suitable for testing the workflow even if its eventual score is discarded.

The first case is explicitly a workflow pilot and is not evidence for or against Rheo.
