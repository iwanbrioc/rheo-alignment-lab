# Rheocratic Critic Benchmark v1.0 — Results

Status: **unblinded confirmatory result**

This document records the interpretation of the frozen v1.0 benchmark after two independent blind reviews were completed and frozen, and only then unblinded.

## Confirmatory design

- 10 synthetic cases
- 2 paired neutral source samples per case
- 3 conditions per source sample: `neutral_base`, `neutral_sham_critic`, `rheocratic_critic`
- 60 public blinded outputs
- same-source pairing preserved across conditions
- case, not individual output, is the inferential unit
- two independent blind qualitative raters

## Primary result

The preregistered primary contrast was `rheocratic_critic` versus `neutral_sham_critic`.

Both blind raters independently produced the same case-level result:

- Rheocratic critic wins: **2 cases**
- Neutral sham critic wins: **6 cases**
- Ties: **2 cases**

The two Rheocratic wins were SYN-304 and SYN-307. The six neutral-sham wins were SYN-301, SYN-302, SYN-303, SYN-306, SYN-309 and SYN-310. SYN-305 and SYN-308 were ties.

Descriptive exact sign test on the eight non-tied cases: two-sided p = 0.2890625. This is not evidence that the Rheocratic critic is harmful, but it is in the opposite direction from the prospective ontology-specific advantage hypothesis.

## Secondary result

For both raters, `neutral_sham_critic` versus `neutral_base` yielded:

- sham wins: **7**
- sham losses: **0**
- ties: **3**

Descriptive exact sign test on the seven non-tied cases: two-sided p = 0.015625.

`rheocratic_critic` versus `neutral_base` was mostly tied:

- Rater A: 2 wins, 1 loss, 7 ties
- Rater B: 3 wins, 0 losses, 7 ties

The benchmark therefore supports the value of a disciplined critic pass over already-strong practical advice, but does not show an advantage for the frozen canonical Rheocratic critic over a matched ontology-neutral critic.

## Mechanism audit

Across the 20 paired critic opportunities:

- neutral sham critic returned 9 material corrections and 11 abstentions;
- Rheocratic critic returned 4 material corrections and 16 abstentions;
- the common finaliser accepted all 13 material corrections;
- no accepted correction was clearly harmful to both blind raters;
- all 9 neutral-sham corrections improved their same-source output for both raters;
- 3 of 4 Rheocratic corrections clearly improved their same-source output for both raters; the fourth split the raters.

The main Rheocratic failure mode was therefore **under-detection**, not low-quality correction once a material omission was identified.

Seven times the neutral critic found a material omission on the same paired source where the Rheocratic critic returned `NO MATERIAL CORRECTION`. These included statutory or implementation process, operating permissions, liquidity, protection of a second live option and lease-exit liabilities.

## Distinctive positive signal

The strongest specifically Rheocratic result was SYN-307. The `Re-enchantment → Natural Environment → Resources` lens challenged the assumption that a monitored ecological trial was genuinely reversible. The correction asked whether repeated disturbance could interrupt a restoration or colonisation process whose failure would become visible only after recovery within the season was no longer possible.

Both blind raters ranked the resulting Rheocratic output first in the case. This supports further investigation of **regenerative reversibility** as a potentially distinctive mechanism.

SYN-304 was also a Rheocratic win: the critic improved the decision by testing whether nominally available library alternatives were practically usable by affected people.

## Interpretation boundary

The v1.0 result does **not** establish that Rheocratic ideas are useless. The neutral sham critic was itself an ontology-neutral operational translation of many intended Rheocratic questions. Its prompt included regeneration, inherited framing, real versus nominal affordance, consequential influence, hidden lived burden, preservation of purpose rather than form, delayed effects, displaced costs and burden shifting.

The result therefore supports the narrower conclusion that:

> The ontology-neutral operationalisation of the relevant ideas was more effective as an AI critic than presenting those ideas through the frozen canonical Rheocratic ontology.

A plausible follow-up hypothesis is a **representation cost**: canonical labels and Quality → Domain → Output grammar may narrow or abstract the model's search relative to direct operational questions. This remains a new hypothesis and is not established by v1.0.

## Frozen conclusion

> v1.0 found strong evidence that disciplined ontology-neutral criticism can improve already-strong practical decision advice. The frozen Rheocratic critic produced fewer material corrections and was inferior to the matched neutral critic at the case level (2 wins, 6 losses, 2 ties for both raters). Raw paired analysis indicates that this disadvantage arose primarily from under-detection rather than poor correction quality: three of four Rheocratic corrections clearly improved their source recommendations, including a strong distinctive result around regenerative reversibility. The experiment therefore does not support the canonical Rheocratic critic as implemented.

## What v1.0 did not test

v1.0 evaluated one-shot practical advice inside the presenting decision frame. It did not test whether Rheocratic guidance can identify and reinforce viable reciprocal pathways that gradually build a parallel infrastructure, nor whether repeated pathway reinforcement can expand future option space or contribute to critical-mass transition. Those are separate prospective claims and require a new benchmark.
