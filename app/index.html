Tell Rheo what’s going on

Describe a real situation you are trying to make sense of. Rheo will help you separate what you know from what you are assuming, look at the wider picture, notice what may be getting stuck, and find a next step you can revise.

Rheo is a thinking tool, not an authority. It starts from what you tell it and can get things wrong. It does not diagnose people or replace professional, emergency, medical, legal, safeguarding or financial advice.
About the research version

Rheo is being developed as a Reciprocal Wellbeing decision guide and AI-alignment research instrument. It does not calculate a wellbeing score. Research settings and technical labels are kept out of the main path where possible.

Step 1 · What’s going on?

Start with the situation, not the verdict

Keep in mind: having to decide quickly does not mean the consequences will pass quickly.
Step 2 · What do we actually know?

Separate what happened from what we think it means

Important claims can quietly turn from “this is how I see it” into “this is what happened”. For each important statement, say where it comes from and how sure you are.

Only one side so far: this map currently comes mainly from the account entered here. That does not make it wrong, but it limits what we can safely say about other people’s motives or disputed events.
Step 3 · Look at the bigger picture

What looks blocked, and what seems to be working?

You do not need to fill in everything. Use only the lenses that help you see something you had not noticed before.

About the seven RWB lenses

Internally, Rheo uses seven Reciprocal Wellbeing horizons. They are lenses, not scores, and a problem seen in one place may be caused somewhere else.

Step 4 · What might be getting stuck?

Make a working guess that can change

Try to name the pattern without pretending you already know the cause.

This is optional. The AI review separately checks whether it can see a specific contribution from the narrator.

Power and safety check

These questions do not diagnose anyone. They help distinguish an ordinary disagreement from a situation where somebody may not be free or safe to disagree, leave, or say no.

Not enough information yet: unanswered safety questions are not proof that everything is safe. You can continue, but keep the uncertainty visible.
Take extra care here: do not assume that more dialogue, disclosure, mediation or confrontation is safe when someone may fear retaliation or may not be free to leave or refuse.
Step 5 · What could be hard to undo?

Protect the possibility of changing course later

Ask: does this limit preserve future choices, or only preserve the way things are now?
Step 6 · What could you try?

Open up options before forcing an answer

Think of several possible moves. A move can solve your immediate problem and still create a cost for someone else, the environment, or the future.

Your working plan

About the plan structure

The report uses SMEAC — Situation, Mission, Execution, Administration and Command/Signal — as a practical way to turn the map into action.
