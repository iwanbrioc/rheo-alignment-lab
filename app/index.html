Rheo
Experimental Reciprocal Wellbeing decision guide
Research prototype v0.2 · Reciprocal Wellbeing · Rheocracy

What is happening?

Describe a real situation. Rheo helps separate observation from interpretation, map relevant restrictions in reciprocal flow, surface missing perspectives and displaced costs, and turn the working map into an actionable SMEAC.

This is not an authority or assessment. Rheo begins from one narrator's account unless other evidence is supplied. It does not calculate a Reciprocal Wellbeing score, diagnose people, or replace professional, emergency, medical, legal, safeguarding or financial advice.
Step 1 · Context

Start with the situation, not a verdict

Temporal check: A short decision deadline does not shorten the recovery time of the system affected by the decision.
Step 2 · Epistemic provenance

What kind of claim is each important statement?

Fluent summaries can quietly turn interpretation into fact. Keep consequential propositions attached to their source.

Single-narrator warning: At present this map is built only from the account entered here. That does not make it false, but it limits what can safely be concluded about absent people's motives or disputed events.
Step 3 · The seven horizons

Where does restriction appear?

These are lenses, not scores. Mark only what is relevant. A restriction can appear in one horizon while being generated elsewhere.

Step 4 · Working contraction & power

Form a hypothesis that can be wrong

This event is logged separately in the research prototype so later attrition is not mistaken for evidence that agreeable advice works better.

Power / safety gate

These are uncertainty flags, not diagnoses. If safety, coercion or constrained exit may be involved, dialogue and relationship preservation are not default goals.

Safety gate active: Do not use relationship recovery time as an argument for continued exposure to danger or coercion. Increased dialogue, disclosure, mediation or confrontation should not be assumed safe.
Step 5 · Time, regeneration and viability

Constrain irreversibility, not emergence

Temporal direction: Does a proposed constraint preserve future possibility, or merely preserve the existing order?
Step 6 · Possible moves

Increase affordance before imposing behaviour

Generate several possible moves. A locally successful move can still fail if somebody outside the immediate frame pays the cost.

Rheocratic SMEAC

A working map, not a verdict. Act, observe, revise.
