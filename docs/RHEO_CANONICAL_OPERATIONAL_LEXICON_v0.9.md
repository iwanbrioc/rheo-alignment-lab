# Rheo Canonical Operational Lexicon v0.9

## Status

Prospective design document for the v0.9 Rheo implementation.

This document is intended to preserve the canonical Reciprocal Wellbeing ontology while making its terms operational in practical decision support. It is **not** an empirical validation of the ontology and does not change the frozen v0.8 benchmark.

## Source / interpretation discipline

Source basis: *The Reciprocal Wellbeing Model* (Iwan Brioc, 2024).

The source model presents seven reciprocal triplets:

- Re-enchantment ↔ Natural Environment ↔ Resources
- Transformation ↔ Culture ↔ Values
- Creativity ↔ Infrastructure ↔ Affordance
- Dialogue ↔ Society ↔ Support
- Curiosity ↔ Outer Self ↔ Capacity
- Participation ↔ Inner Self ↔ Well-being
- Nothing / Everything ↔ No Self ↔ Everything / Nothing

It presents the horizons separately for explanation but describes the model as ecological, cyclical and reciprocal rather than as a literal linear input/output pipeline. Restrictions are understood as restrictions in reciprocal flow. The v0.9 operational language below is an implementation hypothesis derived from that source. It must remain falsifiable.

## Canonical writing rule

A Rheocratic term must never function as a diagnosis by itself.

Do **not** write:

> Capacity is constrained.
>
> Relocate towards Resources.
>
> Values appear primary.

Every use of the ontology must instead complete this causal grammar:

> **Quality → Domain → Output**
>
> We observe **X**.
>
> This may indicate that **[quality] is restricted in [domain]**, limiting **[output]** in this particular way.
>
> The smallest way to test or release that restriction is **Y**.
>
> If the hypothesis is right, we should observe **Z**.
>
> If **F** happens instead, revise the map.

A contraction is a working hypothesis, not a diagnosis.

Prefer **release** to **solution** when the intervention is intended to remove a restriction and allow reciprocal flow rather than impose a target state.

# 1. Re-enchantment → Natural Environment → Resources

## Canonical meaning

Re-enchantment is not merely environmental concern. In the source model it is taking the risk of falling in love with life, the natural environment and ourselves as we are. The term **Resources** is deliberately linked to the older sense of *resourdre* — “to rise again” — rather than only to “available assets”. This emphasises resilience, regeneration and reciprocity. The human body is not treated as separate from the natural environment; its capacity to heal and restore is included in this field.

The source warning is therefore deeper than “use fewer resources”: without Re-enchantment, living systems — including ourselves — can be treated primarily as exploitable stock, with control confused with flow and capital confused with wealth.

## Operational meaning

**Re-enchantment changes the relationship between an actor and that which sustains the actor.**

Decision signature:

> Stop treating the sustaining system as stock. Become responsive to its condition and its capacity to rise again.

Possible instances include ecological systems, bodily recovery, staff energy, attention, trust, volunteer goodwill, financial slack or other regenerative conditions. This does not make all of these literally “natural resources”; it asks whether the relationship has become extractive.

## Characteristic contraction

Something life-giving is treated as indefinitely available because deterioration has not yet stopped production.

The system mistakes **availability now** for **regenerative capacity over time**.

## Discriminating questions

- What is sustaining this situation that we are currently treating as expendable?
- How does it recover, renew or replenish?
- Are we drawing from it faster than it can rise again?
- What would become visible if we attended directly to its condition rather than only to what it produces for us?

## Smallest release

Make one consequential action responsive to regeneration.

Example:

> Before designing a rota that assumes more voluntary swaps, check overtime concentration, refusals, vacancies and recovery time. If the same people repeatedly absorb the pressure, stop treating willingness-to-cover as available stock.

## Expected reciprocal effect

Re-enchantment changes the relationship to the sustaining system → resource use becomes responsive to regeneration → future viability and downstream affordances are preserved rather than purchased through hidden depletion.

## Falsifier

If the supposedly depleted resource is demonstrably regenerating within the relevant timescale and its condition does not materially constrain the decision, Re-enchantment is not the operative restriction.

## False friends

Not: conservation branding, compulsory gratitude, austerity, romanticising scarcity, generic “resource management”, or inspirational language about nature.

Distinctively Rheocratic: recognising that what appears to be a resource may actually be a **reciprocal regenerative relationship**.

# 2. Transformation → Culture → Values

## Canonical meaning

Culture in the source model is deeper than arts, customs or explicit beliefs. Culture participates in constructing consensus reality: it makes particular ways of perceiving and acting seem natural. Values emerge within that cultural field and govern how resources are used.

Transformation is therefore not ordinary change-management. The source describes the required change as a gestalt shift in which what seemed given or inevitable becomes visible as one possible construction among others. It also cautions against confusing sustainability with continuity.

## Operational meaning

**Transformation makes the apparently inevitable contingent again.**

Decision signature:

> Find the assumption that is currently experienced as reality rather than as a choice.

The explicit values may be unobjectionable while the generating culture remains restrictive.

## Characteristic contraction

A culturally produced assumption has become invisible because everyone is operating inside it.

Typical language:

- “That is just how it works.”
- “There is no other way.”
- “Obviously the choice is A or B.”
- “Everyone knows...”

## Discriminating questions

- What are we treating as a fact that may actually be a cultural assumption?
- What would have to look different for a different set of values to become possible?
- Whose experience, language or epistemology would make the current framing look strange?
- Are we preserving continuity and calling it sustainability?

## Smallest release

Introduce the smallest sufficiently different experience, comparison or perspective that makes the inherited frame visible.

Example:

> Before accepting that both evening buses must be withdrawn together, cost one well-timed final journey. The two-or-none framing may itself be the inherited assumption.

## Expected reciprocal effect

Transformation makes a culturally “given” assumption visible → valuation can change → different uses of Resources and different Infrastructures become thinkable.

## Falsifier

If changing the frame produces no meaningful difference in valuation or action and the constraint remains genuinely material, look elsewhere.

## False friends

Not: change for its own sake, rebranding, vision statements, ideological conversion, or forcing people to adopt “better values”.

Distinctively Rheocratic: altering the context in which values arise.

# 3. Creativity → Infrastructure → Affordance

## Canonical meaning

Infrastructure is broad in the source model: physical and digital structures, links between people and institutions, and embodied pathways through which action and perception become possible. Culture and Values become embodied in Infrastructure.

Affordance means the actions that are actually possible because the necessary infrastructure exists.

## Operational meaning

**Creativity converts possibility-in-imagination into possibility-in-practice.**

Decision signature:

> An option is not an Affordance unless someone can actually use it.

## Characteristic contraction

People are asked to behave differently while the infrastructure that would make the different behaviour possible does not exist.

Examples:

- “Work flexibly” but rotas cannot be exchanged.
- “Use public transport” but the train arrives after the last bus.
- “Speak up” but dissent has career consequences.
- “Choose the smaller option” but no temporary mechanism exists through which it can be tried.

## Discriminating questions

- What action do people currently want or need to take but cannot actually take?
- What is physically, institutionally, socially or informationally missing?
- What is the smallest thing we could create or alter that would make the action real?
- Is the supposed option accessible to the people who are expected to use it?

## Smallest release

Build the **minimum viable affordance**: a connection, permission, protected channel, timetable alteration, prototype, contractual clause, small fund, shared map or mechanism.

Do not build a whole programme when one enabling structure would test the hypothesis.

## Expected reciprocal effect

Creativity changes Infrastructure → a previously unavailable action becomes possible → the new Affordance permits behaviour and learning that could not occur under the previous structure.

## Falsifier

If the structure exists but affected people still cannot realistically take the action, it was nominal affordance rather than actual Affordance.

## False friends

Not: brainstorming, novelty, ideation, an “innovation workshop”, or artistic production as such.

Distinctively Rheocratic: creativity is successful when **the possible changes**.

# 4. Dialogue → Society → Support

## Canonical meaning

The source model describes Society as persistent social interaction, with genuine sociality implying mutual support. Society supports people to access Affordance and participate in reshaping Infrastructure.

Dialogue is not merely multiple people speaking. The source invokes *dia/logos* — flow of meaning — and places Dialogue at an important turning point in reciprocal flow.

## Operational meaning

**Dialogue exists when meaning can move through the social system and change what the system does.**

Twenty meetings with no influence can contain less Dialogue than one consequential conversation.

## Characteristic contraction

Meaning cannot travel because, for example:

- someone cannot speak safely;
- roles pre-script the conversation;
- one party can hear but need not respond;
- consultation happens after the decision;
- fixed identities determine what each side is allowed to mean;
- voice exists without exit, power or consequence.

## Discriminating questions

- Whose meaning cannot currently enter the decision?
- Who can speak but cannot influence?
- What might we discover here that none of the parties can state in advance?
- What would have to be genuinely open for this to count as Dialogue rather than consultation?

## Smallest release

Open one consequential channel of meaning.

Example:

> Before finalising the care contract, reconstruct one actual week of routes and let workers confidentially correct the assumptions. Their account must be capable of changing the price, geographic boundary or decision to proceed.

## Expected reciprocal effect

Dialogue allows meaning to move rather than merely be transmitted → Society can provide more appropriate Support → people can access and reshape Affordance → lived information returns into Infrastructure and Culture.

## Falsifier

If people contribute meaning but the relevant decision cannot change, the intervention is not releasing Dialogue.

## False friends

Not: consultation volume, meetings, consensus, debate, or mediation-by-default.

Do not equate conversation volume with Dialogue where power, retaliation or constrained exit make influence illusory.

Distinctively Rheocratic: Support emerges from **flow of meaning with consequence**.

# 5. Curiosity → Outer Self → Capacity

## Canonical meaning

The source model treats Curiosity as a basis for Dialogue: the capacity to keep asking why, seek causes and detect patterns. It describes forms of “curiosity fatigue” in which complexity or precarity produces either fixed narratives that answer questions immediately or apathy in which questions are no longer asked.

The Outer Self concerns how we appear and act in the world — roles, identities, vocation and social location. Curiosity is presented as a driver of Capacity; fixed identity and premature certainty can narrow what a person or system can do.

## Operational meaning

**Curiosity interrupts premature closure.**

Decision signature:

> Ask the question whose answer changes what is worth doing.

This is not a licence for endless research.

## Characteristic contraction

The action tree has been pruned by an assumption nobody has tested.

Examples:

- “The deadline is fixed.”
- “The customer requires every feature.”
- “The council requires this particular form of saving.”
- “The authority will not pay travel time.”

## Discriminating questions

- What have we already decided is true?
- Which uncertainty, if answered, would remove the most unnecessary work?
- What is the cheapest way of finding out?
- What answer would cause us to change course?

The final question prevents Curiosity becoming indiscriminate information gathering.

## Smallest release

Ask one **decision-changing question**, preferably at the cheapest point in the sequence.

Example:

> Before spending scarce engineering time on a detailed delivery plan, ask the customer which requirements are actually mandatory for signature and which can follow go-live.

## Expected reciprocal effect

Curiosity reopens an assumption → Outer Self/system can respond rather than repeat a script → Capacity expands → more viable Affordances remain possible.

## Falsifier

If the answer cannot materially alter the action tree, it is not useful Curiosity for this decision.

## False friends

Not: endless research, scepticism for its own sake, analysis, or asking lots of questions.

Distinctively Rheocratic: Curiosity generates **Capacity to respond differently**.

# 6. Participation → Inner Self → Well-being

## Canonical meaning

Participation in this horizon does not primarily mean stakeholder or democratic participation. The source contrasts the Outer Self question “Who are you?” with the Inner Self question “What is it like to be you?”. Participation means participation in lived inner experience.

Without such participation, apparent wellbeing can become coping or pacification rather than a genuinely inhabited life.

## Operational meaning

**Participation makes first-person lived experience causally available to the system.**

Dialogue asks:

> What meaning needs to move between us?

Participation asks:

> What is this actually like from inside?

## Characteristic contraction

The system is functioning according to external indicators while those living inside it are adapting through coping, numbness, compliance or hidden depletion.

Examples may include productivity hiding exhaustion, absence of complaint being mistaken for wellbeing, or adaptation masking loss of meaningful options.

Do not pathologise acceptance or serenity merely because conditions are difficult.

## Discriminating questions

- What is this actually like to live through?
- What happens in the body, attention, mood or sense of agency when this arrangement operates?
- Does the external account of success correspond with the lived account?
- What are people having to stop feeling, notice or do in order to make the arrangement tolerable?

## Smallest release

Bring first-person evidence into a decision where only external performance has been counted.

Example:

> Before assuming a rota works because every shift is filled, ask the people repeatedly covering the gaps whether the arrangement is recoverable and what it is costing them.

## Expected reciprocal effect

Participation makes lived experience available → Well-being becomes an inhabited signal rather than an external attribution → Capacity and Dialogue can respond to what life inside the system is actually like.

## Falsifier

If relevant affected people report no meaningful experiential burden, this remains consistent over time, and independent evidence does not show hidden depletion or coercion, do not invent an Inner Self contraction merely because the model expects one.

## False friends

Not: attendance, stakeholder participation, democratic participation, “engagement”, or happiness scoring.

Distinctively Rheocratic: **participation in experience itself**.

# 7. Nothing / Everything → No Self → Everything / Nothing

## Canonical meaning

The source model treats selfhood as a useful but energetically costly construction maintained through continuing “selfing”. It links moments of reduced self-consciousness with flow and describes No Self as a horizon through which the inner/outer and giving/receiving distinctions cease to be ultimate.

No Self operates expansively across the other horizons rather than simply as another scoreable domain.

## Operational meaning

**Loosen the requirement that the present self, role, institution, boundary or problem-definition remain the centre around which the decision is organised.**

This is an orientation, not a target state.

No Self must not be reduced to a generic “context breadth” score. Earlier simulation work found no support for the specific `context_breadth` operationalisation; that implementation was too conservative or mis-specified.

## Characteristic contraction

The system increasingly spends effort defending an identity or boundary whose preservation has become confused with the purpose of the system.

Examples:

- “The organisation must survive.”
- “I must win.”
- “Our department must retain control.”
- “The relationship must continue.”
- “This is my problem.”
- “We have to do something.”

The compulsion to intervene can itself be content orientation.

## Discriminating questions

- What has become the unquestioned centre of this map?
- What are we working to preserve because we have identified ourselves with it?
- What becomes visible if that identity or boundary is not treated as primary?
- Is there actually anything that needs doing here, or are we generating action to maintain the sense of being the actor?

## Smallest release

Depending on the case: redistribute agency, relinquish unnecessary control, widen the boundary of concern, allow self-organisation, or deliberately refrain from premature intervention.

No Self must **never** be used to tell another person to surrender, dissolve boundaries, accept abuse, relinquish rights or become “less egoic”.

## Expected reciprocal effect

Less effort is spent defending the privileged centre → previously invisible relationships become perceptible → action becomes responsive to the wider context rather than to preservation of the actor → reciprocal flow becomes possible across horizons.

## Falsifier

If decentring the current frame reveals no new causal relationship, changes no relevant action and merely adds philosophical complexity or caution, No Self has contributed nothing to this decision and should disappear from the output.

## False friends

Not: selflessness, self-sacrifice, passivity, spiritual superiority, boundarylessness, denying agency, “seeing all perspectives”, or generic systems thinking.

Distinctively Rheocratic: the possibility that **the apparent centre around which the problem is organised is itself part of the problem**.

# Whole-cycle use

The seven horizons are not seven boxes to complete. Rheo should construct a **working reciprocal map** only from horizons that materially change the understanding of the case.

Example causal flow:

> Re-enchantment → Resources: repeated cover may be treating staff energy as endlessly available rather than regenerative.
>
> Transformation → Values: “visible saving” may have been culturally interpreted as requiring a visible service cut.
>
> Creativity → Affordance: an alternative rota cannot matter unless the infrastructure makes swaps feasible.
>
> Dialogue → Support: staff and affected users need a consequential route into the design.
>
> Curiosity → Capacity: first ask the council what forms of saving actually qualify.
>
> Participation → Well-being: check what repeated evening cover is actually like for the people absorbing it.
>
> No Self: do not make preservation of the current timetable or management's preferred solution the centre of the problem.

Then ask:

> **Where is the smallest release with the greatest plausible effect on reciprocal flow?**

The answer may be very simple. A simple action is not less Rheocratic if it follows from the map.

# The Seven Ways to Wellbeing

The Ways to Wellbeing should not be forced into one-to-one correspondence with the seven horizons and should not be treated as seven mandatory interventions.

Canonical Ways:

- Be Active
- Be Creative
- Connect
- Keep Learning
- Take Notice
- Give
- Let Go

Operational role in v0.9:

> **The horizons help identify where/how reciprocal flow may be restricted. The Ways supply candidate verbs through which a release might occur.**

Use only the Way or Ways that materially contribute to the smallest sufficient release.

# Required v0.9 action grammar

For every proposed Rheocratic intervention that relies on a horizon, internally represent:

```text
Horizon: [Quality → Domain → Output]

Observed restriction:
[Concrete observation or uncertainty]

Rheocratic hypothesis:
[How the reciprocal process may be restricted]

Discriminating question:
[Cheapest question/observation capable of changing the map]

Smallest release:
[Concrete reversible action]

Expected reciprocal effect:
[What should change if the hypothesis is right]

Falsifier:
[What result would make the current map wrong or materially incomplete]
```

## Example

```text
Horizon: Curiosity → Outer Self → Capacity

Observed restriction:
The team is treating the customer's stated feature list as fixed.

Rheocratic hypothesis:
Premature closure of Curiosity is consuming engineering Capacity.

Discriminating question:
Which requirements are actually mandatory for signature, and which can follow go-live?

Smallest release:
Ask the customer within 24 hours and do no detailed estimation until the answer is received.

Expected reciprocal effect:
Clarification removes unnecessary work → Capacity increases → more viable technical Affordances remain available.

Falsifier:
The customer confirms every requirement is mandatory and non-stageable.
```

# Non-negotiable implementation rule

**Never name a Rheocratic term without immediately translating it into an observable relationship, a discriminating question, a concrete action implication, or a falsifiable prediction.**

The canonical terminology remains intact. What changes in v0.9 is the requirement that the terms behave as causal movements rather than free-standing category labels.
