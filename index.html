Why Rheo

Most decisions aren’t really one decision.

Ordinary advice often collapses a predicament into a verdict: choose A or B, optimise a score, follow the recommendation.

Real decisions are messier. There is usually an immediate problem to handle, something important you still do not know, and a wider set of conditions that could change what becomes possible next.

Rheo keeps those jobs separate, so a decision can solve what matters now without quietly closing off better routes later.

01

Handle the present

Protect safety, deadlines, affordability and live options before chasing more speculative gains.

02

Reduce uncertainty

Find the decisive question, test an assumption, or establish a baseline instead of gathering information indefinitely.

03

Increase possibility

Look for small, testable moves that can create new capability, relationships or future choices without overcommitting now.

How it works

Start with the predicament. Keep the process ordinary.

Rheo is designed around a short decision loop rather than a questionnaire, score or personality test.

1

Describe what you’re trying to work out

Write the situation in your own words, including the pressures, constraints or trade-offs that matter.

2

Add local context only if it helps

Nearby places or services can be treated as possibilities to check. Location is optional, not a condition of using Rheo.

3

Get three genuinely different moves

One acts now, one learns, and one opens a pathway. They are not three phrasings of the same recommendation.

4

Choose what you will actually do

You can choose one of Rheo’s actions, choose something else, or choose not yet. The recommendation is advice; the choice stays explicit.

Built around a different idea of good advice

Useful now. More possible next.

The name Rheo points to flow: decisions are moves through changing conditions, not final judgements made from a frozen snapshot.

Human choice stays explicit

Rheo can reveal routes and consequences. It does not turn a recommendation into a command.

Immediate competence comes first

Urgent safety, legality, deadlines, affordability and live options should not be sacrificed for an elegant long-term theory.

Burden has to be visible

A pathway is not “better” if it quietly depends on unpaid labour, coordination, maintenance, subsidy or risk that nobody has named.

Nearby does not automatically mean better

Local, cooperative, community or voluntary options are evidence to test, not moral endorsements.

Prefer traversable increments

Smaller moves can be tried, observed and revised. That is often more useful than committing early to a grand solution.

Privacy by design

Your location is context, not a tracking feed.

The current alpha is being built without accounts, cloud sync, analytics SDKs, background location or movement history.

If you choose to use local context, location is foreground-only and reduced before it is used. Raw coordinates are not stored in the saved decision session.

Rheo Alpha

Deliberately modest to begin with.

Rheo is in active development. The first product is intentionally focused: describe a predicament, optionally add local context, receive three ways forward, make an explicit choice, and save the decision locally.

No social feed. No moral score. No hidden ranking of people. No map of where you have been.
