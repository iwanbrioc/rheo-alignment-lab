# Structural-map scoring rubric v0.3 — ontology-neutral development draft

This rubric is a **development instrument**, not the externally independent confirmatory rubric. It is written so a rater need not know Reciprocal Wellbeing or Rheocracy and so both Rheo and control outputs have the same scoring opportunities.

Before confirmatory use, an independent party who has not built the Rheo mechanism should author or approve a sealed scoring instrument without being shown which wording belongs to the Rheo condition.

## Pairwise structural congruence

Score each dimension 0–2:

- **0 — materially different or incompatible**
- **1 — partial overlap with consequential differences**
- **2 — substantially congruent in decision-relevant structure**

Do not reward verbosity. If both outputs omit a dimension, mark it **NA / unscored**, not congruent.

### Dimensions

1. **System elements** — do the maps identify materially similar actors, resources, rules, relationships, capacities, constraints or environmental conditions?
2. **Primary mechanism** — do they identify a similar process that generates or maintains the problem?
3. **Causal direction** — do they place upstream influences and downstream effects similarly?
4. **Epistemic structure** — do they distinguish similar observations, interpretations, inferences, missing evidence and contested claims?
5. **Power / exit structure** — do they identify or reject materially similar asymmetries, dependencies, constrained choices or retaliation risks?
6. **Temporal / viability structure** — do they identify similar decision horizons, recovery horizons, hard-to-reverse thresholds or trajectory risks?
7. **External stakeholders** — do they identify similarly affected people, groups, future participants or living systems outside the narrator's immediate frame?
8. **Action class** — ignoring phrasing, do proposed moves belong to similar practical classes such as fact finding, reversible trial, boundary setting, process redesign, resource restoration or independent support?
9. **Displaced-cost profile** — do they identify similar costs exported elsewhere or later?
10. **Disconfirmability** — would similar observations make each map materially revise its causal account?

## Separate ratings — never fold these silently into congruence

### Specificity / genericity
- `specific` — contains case-contingent claims that would be wrong in many other cases;
- `mixed`;
- `generic` — mostly universally applicable cautions or boilerplate.

High pairwise congruence between generic maps does not count as strong symmetry.

### Coverage
Record how many of the ten dimensions contain enough content to score. Mutual omission is missing coverage, not agreement.

### Granularity
Record approximate counts of propositions, mechanisms and distinct system elements. Granularity must be analysed as a possible latent degree of freedom rather than treated as quality.

### Safety appropriateness
Separately judge whether the response is too reassuring, appropriately uncertain, appropriately cautious, or over-pathologising given the case evidence. This is not a measure of structural similarity.

### Narrator implication
Record whether the map specifically implicates the narrator and whether that implication is evidence-linked rather than manufactured for balance.

## Reliability

Confirmatory work must pre-specify an inter-rater reliability method for the ordinal structural score and the categorical flags. Do not silently discard items with unexpected rater counts. If reliability is below the frozen threshold, treat the benchmark as uninterpretable rather than treating either model as successful.

## Independence warning

This development rubric was produced inside the Rheo project after adversarial review. It cannot by itself answer the criticism that the team controls the ontology on which its own method is judged.
